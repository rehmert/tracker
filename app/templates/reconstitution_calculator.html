    Understanding reconstitution calculations

    Reference arithmetic for reviewing your entries. This is not a dose or preparation recommendation.

    Total amount, concentration, and liquid volume

    A vial's total content (for example, 5 mg) is different from its concentration (mg/mL). Concentration equals total content divided by the final solution volume.

    Adding more liquid lowers concentration and increases the volume needed to measure the same entered amount. It does not increase the compound present.

    The water added and final solution volume may differ because of powder displacement. A nominal volume from the reverse calculator is not an exact mixing instruction.

    Unit conversions and U-100 markings
    Mass1 mg = 1,000 mcg
    Liquid1 mL = 100 U-100 syringe marks
    1 mark0.01 mL on a U-100 syringe
    IUBiological activity, not interchangeable with mg or syringe marks

    U-100 0.3 mL, 0.5 mL, and 1 mL syringes use the same volume-per-mark scale, but capacity and graduation increments differ. Do not use these markings with a U-40 syringe.

    Calculating a draw and checking a blend

    Draw volume (mL) = entered component amount ÷ that component's concentration (amount/mL). U-100 marks = draw volume × 100.

    In a premixed blend, each component has its own concentration in the same liquid. A single withdrawal contains a proportional amount of every component. Verify each component separately.

    Remainder versus physical waste

    Complete theoretical draws = total vial amount ÷ entered amount, rounded down. If the division leaves a remainder, changing the dilution cannot eliminate that compound remainder.

    Actual recoverable yield can be smaller because of vial hold-up, syringe dead space, transfers, and handling. Optimizer results are nominal mathematical comparisons, not verified yields.

    Common errors to check
    • Mixing up mg and mcg (a factor of 1,000)
    • Confusing biological IU with U-100 volume marks
    • Entering the BAC-water bottle size rather than the actual vial liquid volume
    • Using old marks after changing the final volume or concentration
    • Rounding fractional marks as if it cannot change the amount
    • Assuming a blend contains equal amounts of every component
    Product instructions and storage matter

    Follow the product or pharmacy instructions for the appropriate diluent, preparation technique, compatibility, storage, and beyond-use period. There is no single safe mixing, storage, or expiry rule for every substance.

    Use aseptic handling and record actual measured quantities through Tracker's vial workflow; this calculator never changes inventory.

    Worked arithmetic example (not a suggested dose)

    5 mg in 2 mL nominal final volume gives 2.5 mg/mL. A hypothetical 250 mcg (= 0.25 mg) amount corresponds to 0.1 mL, or 10 U-100 marks. The vial contains 20 complete theoretical amounts of this size.
